The Ben Johnson Effect: Why the Bears are Finally Ready to Bury Green Bay

The North Remembers: Ben Johnson’s Chicago Revolution Meets Its Ultimate Test
For nearly thirty years, the Chicago-Green Bay rivalry hasn't been a rivalry—it’s been a recurring nightmare for the city of Chicago. But as the sun sets on Soldier Field this Saturday, the air feels different. The 'Monsters of the Midway' are no longer a defensive relic; they are an offensive juggernaut led by the man who spent three years systematically dismantling Green Bay from across the lake: Ben Johnson.
The Detroit Defector vs. The Green Bay Standard

When Ben Johnson was hired in January 2025, he didn't just bring Caleb Williams a playbook; he brought a history of psychological warfare. "I kinda enjoyed beating Matt LaFleur twice a year," Johnson famously quipped during his intro. He’s backed it up. In his first year, he transformed a 5-win team into an 11-win division champion. Now, he faces a Packers squad that is technically superior in playoff reps but emotionally fractured.
The Shadow of Week 16

The Packers are playing with a chip on their shoulder the size of the Frozen Tundra. Three weeks ago, in this very stadium, Bears DE Austin Booker delivered the hit that sent Jordan Love into the concussion protocol and sent Green Bay into a four-game tailspin. While Love is cleared to play, the narrative of 'protection' has taken over. Josh Jacobs and the Packers' offensive line have spent the week talking about 'defending their brother.' This isn't just about a win; it's about a reckoning.
Why This Time is Different

Under the previous regime of Matt Eberflus (who was just fired as the Cowboys' DC after a historic defensive collapse), the Bears played with fear. Under Johnson, they play with math. Chicago leads the league with a +22 turnover differential. They aren't waiting for Green Bay to make a mistake; they are designing them.
With Micah Parsons out for the season, the Packers' pass rush is toothless. Caleb Williams, fresh off a franchise passing record, finally has his full arsenal with Rome Odunze and DJ Moore cleared. The king of the North hasn't changed yet, but the throne has never been this shaky.
> HEATCHECKS EDGE
The 'Green Bay Hex' ends Saturday night. While Jordan Love has the experience, he is walking into a hornet's nest without his best protector (Parsons) and against a coach in Ben Johnson who treats LaFleur’s defense like a Sunday crossword puzzle. The Bears' +22 turnover differential isn't a fluke—it's a predatory system. Caleb Williams makes his playoff debut by exorcising thirty years of demons. Take the Bears ML (+100); the emotional tide in Chicago is too strong to ignore.
